Interdisciplinary learning

One big question. Four connected classrooms.

IS English, Theory of Knowledge, CS Course and EFS Sustainable Development approach the same issue from different angles. Switch terms to see two examples.

Why do we read?

Reading becomes a question about knowledge, memory, freedom, empathy and access—not merely pages completed.

IS English

Language & literacy

Compare print and visual narratives, infer meaning, discuss empathy and build evidence-based writing.

Theory of Knowledge

How stories create knowledge

Examine whether memory, language and personal perspective make knowledge reliable or selective.

CS Course · The Giver

Memory, choice & society

Connect the novel’s treatment of shared memory and controlled knowledge with the unit’s inquiry into reading.

EFS Sustainable Development

Access & informed communities

Explore how literacy, inclusive education and access to trustworthy information support responsible participation.

Shared outcome: a “Knowledge, Memory and Choice” inquiry exhibition combining textual evidence, TOK questions and a sustainability perspective.

Is speed important?

Students investigate when speed improves life—and when it weakens attention, judgement or long-term sustainability.

IS English

Balanced argument

Analyse modern pace, technology and transport, then present a balanced for-and-against position.

Theory of Knowledge

Fast information, careful knowledge

Question whether faster access produces better understanding and how time pressure changes evidence evaluation.

CS Course · Fahrenheit 451

Speed, screens & reflection

Connect the novel’s fast, media-saturated society with attention, independent thought and meaningful reading.

EFS Sustainable Development

Fast systems, lasting impact

Evaluate transport, technology and consumption through efficiency, environmental impact and long-term responsibility.

Shared outcome: a “When Should We Slow Down?” future forum where teams evaluate one school or city system and defend a sustainable recommendation.
How we learn

A simple loop, repeated with better questions.

Each unit connects content, communication and reflection. We do more than collect vocabulary: we use English to notice, question, explain and improve.

◉

Explore

Begin with a big question, observe real-world ideas and activate what you already know.

⌁

Read & listen

Find main ideas, evidence, relationships and implied meaning across different kinds of texts.

↗

Speak & write

Turn ideas into discussions, explanations, arguments, reviews and carefully structured writing.

✓

Reflect & improve

Use feedback, revise your choices and transfer each new strategy to the next challenge.

Our learning platforms

Different tools. Different jobs. One learning journey.

Sensations supports differentiated reading routes, while ScriboWriting gives our writing process a clear home from first idea to stronger final draft.

Differentiated reading

Sensations English

Our reading platform helps us offer appropriate levels of support and challenge, so students can approach shared topics through a route suited to their current reading needs.

  • Build reading confidence at an appropriate pace
  • Practise main ideas, details, inference and vocabulary in context
  • Use different reading routes to work toward shared class goals
Writing development

ScriboWriting

Scribo is our writing platform. We use it to make writing visible as a process: generate ideas, organise, draft, respond to feedback, revise and submit with greater control.

  • Plan ideas before writing
  • Develop structure, clarity and language control
  • Use feedback to revise rather than simply finish
Our ultimate target

TOEFL ITP at the end of the year.

The test is our destination, not the whole journey. Throughout the year, the themes, reading pathways, language work and listening practice build the proficiency students will need to approach TOEFL ITP with confidence.

1

Listening Comprehension

Main ideas, details, speaker purpose and meaning in spoken English.

2

Structure & Written Expression

Grammar, sentence structure and recognition of standard written English.

3

Reading Comprehension

Academic reading, inference, organisation, detail and vocabulary in context.

Our TOEFL ITP preparation is organised around these three core areas. View the official ETS preparation overview. Speaking and extended writing remain important parts of our wider course even though they are not the three core Level 1 sections.
